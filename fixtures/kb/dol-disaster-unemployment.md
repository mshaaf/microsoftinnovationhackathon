## What is Disaster Unemployment Assistance (DUA)?
DUA is money for unemployed workers and self-employed people who lived, worked, or were scheduled to work in a disaster area when the President declared a major disaster. It is for people who may not qualify for regular unemployment benefits.

## Who may qualify for DUA?
You may qualify if the disaster caused one of these: you no longer have a job or workplace, you cannot reach your workplace, damage to your workplace stops you from working, you were injured in the disaster and cannot work, or you became head of household because the former head died in the disaster. The state agency decides.

## How long can DUA last?
The Disaster Assistance Period starts the first day of the week after the disaster began. It continues for up to 26 weeks after the date the disaster was declared.

## How much is the weekly benefit?
Amounts follow the state's unemployment law maximums. The minimum is half (50%) of the average benefit amount in the state.

## How do I apply?
The affected state announces when DUA is available. Contact your State Unemployment Insurance agency. If you moved, you can contact the affected state or your current state's agency for help filing a claim.

## Who do I contact at the Department of Labor?
Phone: 1-877-US-2JOBS. Fax: 202-693-2726. TTY is available; contact the Department of Labor for details. The page does not list an application deadline beyond the 26-week period.
