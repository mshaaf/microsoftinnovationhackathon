## Why does FEMA need proof that I lived there?
FEMA needs to confirm that you lived at the address on your application as your main home before the disaster. Homeowners asking for repair or replacement help also need to show they own the home. FEMA usually checks this in public records when you apply. If that check does not work, FEMA asks you for documents.

## What documents can show I lived there (occupancy)? I rent.
FEMA accepts one of these:
- Lease or housing agreement
- Rent receipts
- Utility bills
- Employer statement or pay stub
- Bank or credit card statement
- Driver's license, state ID, or voter registration card
- Papers from a public official, medical provider, or social service group
- Motor vehicle registration
- A sworn statement of residency or court papers
- School papers listing the damaged home
- A letter from a mobile home park manager

Most documents can be dated within one year before the disaster or within the 18-month assistance period. A driver's license, state ID, or voter card must be from before the disaster and not expired when you send it.

## What documents show I own the home?
FEMA accepts one of these: deed or official record, mortgage papers, homeowners insurance papers, property tax bill or receipt, manufactured home title, purchase contract, will or heirship papers with a death certificate, receipts for major repairs from the 5 years before the disaster, court papers, or a letter from a mobile home park owner or public official.

## What if I cannot find these documents?
If you lived in a mobile home, travel trailer, or on an island or tribal land, you may send a signed written statement as a last resort. It must give the address, how long you lived there, and say under penalty of perjury that you tried in good faith to get documents. For inherited property, add the previous owner's death certificate.

## How do I send my documents?
The page says the fastest and easiest way is to upload them online at DisasterAssistance.gov.
