## Why might my letter say "not approved"?
The page lists common reasons: missing documents, insurance or other funds, more than one application, ownership not verified, identity or occupancy not verified, not your primary residence, not enough damage, or FEMA could not reach you for the inspection. The page was released June 9, 2025, about a 2025 storm disaster.

## What if FEMA asks for more documents?
Read your letter closely and send FEMA what it asks for. If you do not have something, tell FEMA why.

## What about insurance or other money?
FEMA cannot give money for the same disaster-caused need that you got money for from another source. You can send papers that show the other money was not enough or was for something else.

## Why does FEMA say only one application per household?
"FEMA allows only one application per household or address." Put all household members' losses in one application. To report suspected fraud, call the FEMA Helpline at 800-621-3362 (6 a.m. to 10 p.m. CT daily).

## What proves I own my home?
Deed, title, mortgage or insurance statements, property tax bills, manufactured home certificate, purchase contracts, or a will or death certificate.

## What counts as my primary residence and as damage?
Your primary residence is where you live more than six months of the year. Damage to non-essential areas, landscaping, or spoiled food does not count. FEMA gives grants only for repairs to make a home safe, accessible, and functional.

## What if I missed the inspector's call?
Answer FEMA calls, even from unknown numbers, and go to scheduled appointments. To schedule an inspection, call 800-621-3362.

## Can I appeal?
Yes. You can dispute any decision or award amount. Send receipts, bills, repair estimates, or property deeds. Upload to your DisasterAssistance.gov account, mail to FEMA, P.O. Box 10055, Hyattsville, MD 20782-8055, fax to 800-827-8112, or visit a survivor assistance location.
