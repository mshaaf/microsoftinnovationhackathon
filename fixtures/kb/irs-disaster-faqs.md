## What if I lost my tax records in a disaster?
The IRS says it "will consider documentation requirements satisfied by the best reasonably available information presented in good faith."

## Who do I call about disaster tax questions?
The IRS disaster hotline is 866-562-5227.

## Can I deduct disaster losses on my taxes?
For tax years 2018 through 2025, casualty or theft losses of personal-use property are deductible for individuals only if the loss is due to a federally declared disaster. The deduction is the smaller of two amounts: the adjusted basis of the property minus insurance, or the drop in fair market value minus insurance. Form 4684 is used for casualties and thefts.

## Can I use repair costs to figure my loss?
Yes, if the repairs were actually made, are needed to restore the property, are not excessive, fix only the damage, and do not raise the property's value above what it was before the disaster.

## How are trees and landscaping treated?
Trees are part of the whole residential property and are not valued separately. Use appraisals of the whole property before and after the damage, or the cost-of-repairs method.

## Do postponed deadlines give me more time to claim a refund?
Yes. The IRS says refund claim periods are extended by "120 days up to 1 year, depending on the relief granted."

## Which forms help after a disaster?
Form 1040-X amends a return; write the disaster name at the top. Form 4506 requests a copy of a return; the $50 fee is waived for disaster victims. Form 4506-T requests a transcript, with free expedited service. Form 8822 changes your address. See IRS Publications 547 and 584.
