## What changed about flexible funding?
FEMA added "Serious Needs" help for essentials like food, water, baby formula, breastfeeding supplies, medication, and other emergency supplies. It also added displacement help for temporary housing, such as hotels, staying with family or friends, or rentals.

## Do I have to apply for an SBA loan first?
No. FEMA removed the rule that you must apply for an SBA loan before FEMA looks at your case. You can pursue both at the same time.

## What changed about insurance and repairs?
Homeowners who are underinsured (insurance paid too little) may now qualify for FEMA help. Home repair help now covers pre-existing damage and improvements that guard against future disasters. There is new money for accessibility work such as ramps, grab bars, and paved paths.

## What changed for self-employed people and computers?
Self-employed applicants may get money for disaster-damaged tools and equipment. Computer coverage now includes personal and family computers, plus devices for work, school, or accessibility.

## What got simpler?
- Less paperwork for temporary housing.
- Late applications no longer need a written explanation.
- Appeals no longer need a signed written letter.
- DisasterAssistance.gov was redesigned to cut registration time by more than 15%.
- The Transitional Sheltering Assistance website has photos, mobile use, and better filters.
